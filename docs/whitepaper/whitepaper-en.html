1. Executive Summary

“The most powerful force in Islamic giving is trust. We are not replacing that trust — we are giving it proof.”— Akbar Amirzoda, Founder & CEO

Zakat is the third pillar of Islam: 2.5% of qualifying wealth, owed every lunar year by each Muslim whose wealth exceeds the nisab. The World Bank and IRTI/IsDB estimate the annual Zakat obligation — what is owed, not what is collected — at $50–600 billion. Less than $25 billion of it moves through formal institutions. The rest is given in cash, through intermediaries, or not at all — and almost none of it can be traced by the person who gave it.

AM Network is building the infrastructure that lets a donor see their Zakat arrive: every recipient assessed by AI and confirmed in person by a trusted local verifier, every transfer recorded on a public blockchain, and 100% of the Zakat itself reaching the recipient.

What we are building

AM Zakat.ai — the core platform. A 0–100 AI need score, human confirmation by a local oracle network (mosque imams, AM Network volunteers, partner charities), and escrow smart contracts on the Base blockchain that release funds only after two independent verifiers agree.

AM Academy — free, Sharia-compliant financial literacy. Live today: 5 modules, 20 lessons, every claim linked to a source, AI explanations, and a downloadable completion certificate. NFT certificates are planned.

2. The Problem

2.1 The broken state of Zakat

  • Of an estimated $50–600 billion owed each year, less than $25 billion is distributed through formal institutions.
  • Donors have no reliable way to verify that their Zakat reached an eligible recipient.
  • Many intermediaries publish little or no public accounting.
  • Eligibility checks are manual, slow and hard to audit; fraud and duplicate claims are hard to detect.
  • Many of the people most in need are invisible to formal charitable systems.
“A donor pays Zakat. Three days later — a link. He clicks it and sees: his donation reached a family across the world. Every step is on the blockchain. For the first time, he knows his Zakat arrived.”— The experience AM Network is building. For most donors today, it does not exist.

2.2 Why existing solutions fall short

  • Manual verification of eligibility is slow, subjective and does not scale.
  • Paper documents are easy to forge, and with generative AI, digital ones are too.
  • Funds pass through several hands, with cost and leakage at each step.
  • There is no shared standard for eligibility or for reporting distribution.

3. The Solution — AM Network

“I want to build something beneficial that truly serves the Ummah — a product or system that becomes the reason the Almighty Allah rewards us and has mercy upon us.”— Akbar Amirzoda

AM Network is not a charity. It is infrastructure: the rails on which transparent Islamic giving can move — verifiably and at global scale.

3.1 How it works — step by step

1
Donor givesThe donor calculates their Zakat (our calculator covers 159 countries and the nisab), then gives to a specific verified recipient or to the general Zakat pool. Settlement is in a stablecoin such as USDC.
2
AI scores needRecipients apply privately. The scoring engine rates need 0–100 from family situation, income, health and regional cost of living. No single factor decides. Personal data stays off-chain.
3
Local oracle verifiesA verified local representative — a mosque imam, an AM Network volunteer or a partner charity — confirms the recipient exists and is in need. This human layer catches what AI alone would miss.
4
Escrow releasesFunds wait in a smart contract on Base. They are released only when two distinct oracles confirm delivery. 100% of the Zakat goes to the recipient; the ujrah is charged on top.
5
Donor receives proofEvery step emits a public on-chain event: transaction hash, amount, time, status. The donor gets a link to the record. No personal data is ever written on-chain.

3.2 The AI scoring system

Recipients are registered under one of the eight Zakat categories (asnaf) named in the Quran (9:60). Within them, the scoring model weighs need across 15 groups:

  • Widows and single mothers with dependents
  • Orphans under 18 with no guardian income
  • Elderly people forced to work to survive
  • Disabled people unable to work
  • Families below the local poverty line
  • Students from low-income families
  • People trapped in riba-based debt
  • Refugees and displaced persons
  • The chronically ill without access to healthcare
  • Large families with a single breadwinner
  • Food-insecure households
  • New Muslims facing hardship
  • Survivors of trafficking and forced labour
  • Victims of conflict and natural disasters
  • People in hardship invisible to traditional charity (the list will grow)

Anti-fraud approach

  • Several data sources are cross-checked, so no single document decides.
  • Release needs confirmation by two distinct verified oracles.
  • Oracles post a refundable stake, have per-period release caps, and can be slashed or removed for proven misconduct.
  • Pattern analysis flags inconsistencies across applications.
  • Every release is permanently recorded, so attempted fraud leaves a trail.

Today the scoring quiz on amnetwork.io is a client-side demo of this model. The full scoring engine exists as a prototype and is not yet used for real payouts.

4. Technology Architecture

4.1 Blockchain infrastructure

Base (Coinbase’s Ethereum Layer-2) is our primary chain: Ethereum security at fees of fractions of a cent, which makes small Zakat and Sadaqah payments viable. Solana is the backup for high-volume scenarios, and LayerZero is planned for moving assets between chains.

4.2 Smart contract design (prototype)

Two Solidity contracts are written and tested on a local EVM against OpenZeppelin v5 (29 of 29 checks pass): AMZakatPool for donations to a specific recipient and AMZakatPoolGeneral for the shared pool.

  • Donor’s Zakat goes to escrow allocated to the recipient; the ujrah goes to the treasury. Nothing is taken from the Zakat principal.
  • Tiered ujrah: 2.5% under $1,000, 1.5% for $1,000–10,000, 1.0% from $10,000. A hard cap of 2.5% is enforced in code.
  • A donor sets the maximum fee they accept; the transaction fails rather than charge more.
  • Release needs two distinct oracle confirmations; oracles stake, have release caps and can be slashed.
  • On the shared pool, each oracle key is rate-limited, so one compromised key cannot drain it.
  • Reentrancy guards, safe token transfers and an emergency pause.

Admin powers — disclosed

The contracts have an administrator role. It can pause the contracts in an emergency, manage oracles, adjust fee tiers within the 2.5% cap, and set the treasury. In production this role must be held by a multi-signature wallet, never a single key. We state this plainly because “no one can intervene” would not be true.

Before mainnet: a professional security audit, Sharia board sign-off on the fee mechanics, a multisig admin, and a testnet run on Base Sepolia with real oracle dry-runs. None of these is complete yet.

5. Sharia Compliance

5.1 Wakala bil ujrah

AM Network acts as an agent (wakil) delivering Zakat on the donor’s behalf, for a disclosed service fee (ujrah) agreed in advance. A fee for a legitimate service is not riba. Because the fee is charged on top of the Zakat, nothing is taken from the share owed to the poor.

5.2 No speculative token

We will not issue a speculative cryptocurrency token. Many scholars regard such tokens as impermissible because of gharar (excessive uncertainty) and elements resembling maysir (gambling). Value moves in stablecoins. Our revenue is the service fee, and our growth depends on volume, not token prices.

5.3 Sharia advisory

We are seeking a formal Sharia advisory board of recognized scholars in Islamic finance. A fatwa on our specific ujrah implementation is required before mainnet. We have contacted potential advisors; no one has yet confirmed, and we do not present anyone as an advisor until they have.

  • Planned structure: periodic review and certification of platform changes.
  • All Sharia rulings will be published.

Our free tools do not issue religious rulings. Quran text, translations and recitations are shown live from Quran.com exactly as published; hadith come from an open dataset with sources; any AI translation is labelled as such.
