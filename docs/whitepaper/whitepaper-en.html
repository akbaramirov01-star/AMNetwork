How we got here

1. Summary

“The most powerful force in Islamic giving is trust. We are not replacing that trust — we are helping it find the right person.”— Akbar Amirzoda, Founder

Zakat is the third pillar of Islam: one-fortieth of qualifying wealth, owed every lunar year by each Muslim whose wealth exceeds the nisab. The World Bank and IRTI/IsDB estimate the annual Zakat potential — what is owed, not what is collected — at $50–600 billion. Less than $25 billion of it is distributed through formal institutions. People in need often have no way to reach a giver, and givers often cannot tell which requests are real.

AM Network connects people in need with those who give Zakat and Sadaqah — private donors and charities in any country. AI reads each request and suggests which Zakat category it may fall under and what to check; the giver checks, decides and gives directly. The money never passes through AM Network.

What that means in practice

  • Open to every country. A person in need sends a request at amnetwork.io/apply/, in their own language.
  • AI gives a hint, not a fatwa. AI suggests which of the eight Zakat categories of the Quran (9:60) the request may fit, what to verify, what does not add up and what to ask the applicant. It never sees the applicant’s name, phone, email or address.
  • The giver verifies. Every request carries the label “Not verified by AM Network — the giver verifies.” We do not have our own verifiers yet, and we say so.
  • Contacts with consent only. Givers see requests without contact details. Contact goes only to givers we approved, and only with the applicant’s consent.
  • Optional open report. A gift can be recorded in a public report where each entry carries a SHA-256 fingerprint linked to the one before it, so a quiet change breaks the chain.

Stage

The request form, the AI review and the givers’ area are built (November 2026). We are now writing to charities in all countries. Mobile apps are planned for December 2026. We take nothing from Zakat, there is no token, no income in year one, and we are not raising investment.

Also available

AM Academy — Sharia-compliant financial literacy, explained for four audiences, with a downloadable certificate — and free Islamic tools in 10 languages: Quran, hadith, dua, prayer times, Qibla, Islamic calendar, 99 Names, tasbeeh and a Zakat calculator.

2. The Problem

2.1 Zakat is large, and the two sides rarely meet

  • Of an estimated $50–600 billion owed each year, less than $25 billion is distributed through formal institutions.
  • Many of the people most in need are invisible to formal charity: they do not know whom to ask, they live far from any charity, or nobody speaks for them.
  • Charities and donors receive appeals they cannot easily assess: a story in a language they do not read, no clear link to a Zakat category, no idea what to check first.
  • Copy-pasted mass appeals and invented stories make every giver more cautious, and that caution hurts the honest applicant most.
“A family in one country needs help. A charity in another has Zakat to give. Neither knows the other exists.”— The gap AM Network works on.

2.2 Why it is hard to fix

  • Reading requests takes time. A charity that gets requests from many countries cannot study each one in depth.
  • Eligibility needs knowledge. Whether a situation fits one of the eight Zakat categories is a fiqh question, not only a question of poverty.
  • Documents are easy to forge. With generative AI, digital ones are too. Only the giver, close to the case, can verify in the way that suits them.
  • Every extra hand on the money costs something — fees, delays and room for leakage.
  • Privacy matters. A person asking for help should not have their name and phone number passed around.

AM Network does not try to replace the giver’s judgment. It makes requests easier to find and easier to read, points to what should be checked, protects the applicant’s privacy, and keeps the money out of our hands.

In short: request → AI review → direct help. In detail:

3.1 What AM Network does not do

  • We do not collect, hold, pool or distribute Zakat, and take nothing from it — no account, no escrow, no fee.
  • No algorithm approves or rejects a request, and the AI issues no religious ruling. People decide: the admin decides what is listed, the giver decides whom to help.
  • We share no contact details without the applicant’s consent, and publish nothing that identifies them.

3.2 The label on every request

Every request a giver sees says “Not verified by AM Network — the giver verifies.” We have no verifiers of our own yet, and we do not imply a check we did not make. Later, with income, we plan our own verifiers and a separate mark, “Recommended by the AM Network team”, for requests they have checked. We give no date for that.

4.4 Limits and cost

  • A daily cap kept in the database. The number of reviews per day is capped (30 by default). The cap is counted in the database, not on the server’s disk, so restarting or redeploying the server cannot reset it.
  • Low cost. A review costs roughly one to three US cents. The founder covers it.
  • If the AI is unavailable, the request is still saved, and the admin can run the review later. A request is never lost or rejected because the AI failed.
  • The AI can be wrong. It sees only what was written, it cannot check a single fact, and it may misjudge a category. That is why every review is labelled a hint and every request “Not verified by AM Network”.

5.3 “I want to help” and consent

A giver presses “I want to help” and may add a short message. The admin sees each offer and connects the giver and the applicant by hand. On the form, the applicant answers a separate question: may a charity or donor approved by AM Network contact them, through us, to verify the request? Without that consent, no contact details are passed on. We take no part in the gift itself.

5.4 Rules the database enforces

These rules are written into the database itself, not only into the web page, so a modified page or a direct request cannot get around them:

  • The public form cannot write to the database directly. Requests arrive only through our backend, which holds a shared secret.
  • Nobody but the admin and our backend can set a request’s AI review or whether it is listed.
  • Only approved givers can read the feed or make an offer, and only on a listed, open request. The feed never returns a full name, email, phone or address.
  • A giver sees only their own offers; only the admin sees who offered what.
  • The daily AI cap is checked in the database; only the admin can change roles.

6.2 The SHA-256 hash chain

When a gift is recorded, the database builds one line of text from the entry’s facts and the fingerprint of the entry before it:

payload = prev_hash | ref | amount | currency | kind | date | receipt_sha256
entry_hash = sha256(payload)

The opening entry links to a fingerprint of 64 zeros. Each later entry includes the previous entry’s entry_hash as its prev_hash. Change any past entry, and its fingerprint no longer matches — nor does any after it. The app has no way to edit or delete a recorded entry: entries can only be added.

6.3 Receipts kept as fingerprints

A receipt is never uploaded. The browser computes its SHA-256 fingerprint on the device, and only those 64 characters are stored; showing the original file later proves it is the same receipt.

6.4 Check it yourself

The “Check the chain” button re-checks every entry in the visitor’s own browser; any SHA-256 tool can do the same. The chain proves that a published entry has not been quietly changed: an edit breaks the chain. It does not prove that the money changed hands — that rests on the giver, the recipient and the receipt. Whoever runs the database could in principle rebuild the whole chain, but anyone who saved its latest fingerprint would see the mismatch. So we do not call the report “immutable”.

7. Data Protection

People asking for help trust us with sensitive information: income, health, debts, family. We collect only what a giver needs to understand the request, show it without contact details, and publish none of it.

  • Where it lives. Requests are stored in Supabase (managed PostgreSQL), hosted in the EU region. A copy of each request also reaches our inbox through our form provider (Web3Forms).
  • Row-level security. Every table is locked by default. The database decides, row by row, who may read what — not the web page.
  • Consent. A request cannot be sent without consent to how it is used: stored, reviewed by AI without contact details, shown to approved givers without contact details, kept for up to 24 months. Contact by a giver needs a separate consent. Deletion can be requested at any time at contact@amnetwork.io.
  • The AI provider (Anthropic) receives only the de-identified request described in Section 4.1.
  • No files. Receipts are kept only as fingerprints. The givers’ area uses no analytics and is hidden from search engines.
  • Keys. The key embedded in the page is public by design; it grants nothing that row-level security does not allow. The service key and database password are held by the founder only and never appear in code.

8. Sharia Approach

We follow what scholars broadly agree on and, where they differ, say so openly rather than take sides.

  • Zakat is the giver’s act. Zakat is fulfilled when ownership passes to an eligible recipient (tamlik). In our model the giver hands the Zakat to the recipient directly, or through their own charity, so ownership never passes through us. The intention (niyyah) stays with the giver.
  • We are not an amil. Many scholars hold that a private organisation not appointed by the authorities is not a Zakat collector (amil), and so has no claim on the collectors’ share. Either way, AM Network does not handle the money at all, and takes nothing from Zakat.
  • The category is a suggestion. The AI review suggests which of the eight categories of 9:60 a request may fit. Eligibility — including whether the person has wealth above nisab — is for the giver to establish, with a scholar where needed.
  • Sadaqah is kept separate from Zakat, in the review and in the report, because the rules for the two differ.

Status

We are seeking review of this model by qualified Islamic scholars. AM Network is not certified by any scholar or Sharia board, and we name no one as an advisor until they have agreed.

Our free tools do not issue religious rulings. Quran text, translations and recitations are shown live from Quran.com exactly as published; hadith come from an open dataset with sources; any AI translation is labelled as such.

9. Legal Approach

  • No custody. AM Network never receives, holds or forwards other people’s money. On our reading, it is not a bank, a payment provider or a fund, and needs no money-transfer licence.
  • Givers act under their own rules. A charity that helps through AM Network does so under its own registration and the law of its own country.
  • Personal data. Requests come from many countries. We keep data minimal, hosted in the EU, and based on the applicant’s consent; we will take legal advice as the platform grows.
  • A legal entity for AM Network is planned for later, once there is income to support it, with legal advice.

10. Sustainability

There is no income in year one, and that is a deliberate choice. Costs are small — hosting on free tiers, a few cents of AI per request under a daily cap, and the founder’s time — and the founder covers them.

Later, and only in ways that never touch Zakat

  • Optional tips from givers, shown as a separate line and paid separately — never deducted from Zakat.
  • Grants for transparency and social-impact work.
  • Service fees from charities, paid from their own funds, for the service we provide to them.

Never

  • A fee or percentage taken from Zakat.
  • A token or any speculative instrument.
  • Selling or sharing applicants’ data; advertising.

What income would make possible

Our own verifiers and the “Recommended by the AM Network team” mark; a formal review by qualified Islamic scholars; legal registration. A blockchain record is something we may consider later; we promise nothing about it. We are not raising investment now.

How we got here

Version 1.1 (October 2026) described Zakat moving through escrow smart contracts on the Base blockchain, a 1–2.5% ujrah fee, an “oracle network” of local verifiers including mosque imams, an AI score of 0–100 and a seed round. Version 2.0 (20 November 2026) dropped all of that for a verification and transparency service: our own trained volunteers would visit each family in person, with a first pilot of 10–20 families in Dushanbe around Ramadan 2027, funded only by the founder’s and his relatives’ Zakat.

Version 2.1 (24 November 2026) keeps the principles of 2.0 and changes who checks. We have no volunteers yet and no money to train them, and people in need are not only in one city. So the platform opens now, to every country: AI helps givers read requests, and the giver verifies. What changed:

  • In-person checks by our volunteers → the giver verifies. Every request is labelled “Not verified by AM Network”. Our own verifiers come later, with income.
  • A Dushanbe pilot of 10–20 families → open to all countries. Requests in any language and currency; outreach to charities in all countries starts now.
  • AI is back, as a reading aid. It suggests a 9:60 category, what to verify and red flags; it never sees contact details and decides nothing. The visible need checklist built for volunteers waits for that later stage.
  • The open report becomes optional. The SHA-256 hash chain stays, for givers who want their gift recorded.
  • Unchanged: the money never passes through us; nothing is taken from Zakat; no token, NFT or DAO; no income in year one; not Sharia-certified, a review by qualified Islamic scholars is sought; no partners named; the Zakat potential cited only as a range ($50–600 billion owed, under $25 billion distributed formally). The Solidity code from v1.1 stays in the repository as an archived prototype; it is not used or deployed.
